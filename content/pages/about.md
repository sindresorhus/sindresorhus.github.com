Hi, I’m Sindre. I make apps and open-source software full time. In 2014, I quit my office job to backpack around Southeast Asia while doing open source. The backpacking ended, but the open source never did, and today my work is funded by the community.

Most of my time now goes to [apps](/apps) for Apple devices. They are small, focused tools that solve one problem well, and many of them are free. You can [see what people say about them](/apps/reviews), or [see what I’m working on now](/now).

I also maintain [many npm packages](https://www.npmjs.com/~sindresorhus) and [popular open-source projects](https://github.com/search?q=user:sindresorhus+user:chalk+user:avajs+user:xojs+archived:false&type=Repositories) on GitHub, from Node.js libraries to CLI tools and Swift packages.

## Some Things I Believe

@Cards {
- I’m all in on AI
	- It’s the biggest shift in how software gets made in my lifetime, and I use it every day
	- The bottleneck is no longer writing code. It’s taste, judgment, and knowing what to build
	- AI raises the floor. Taste raises the ceiling
	- With AI, I can make, verify, and ship a fix faster than I can review a pull request for it
	- I still own the result. The design, the decisions, and the quality bar are mine
	- In the AI era, curiosity is the only job security
- Make things for fun, and to solve your own problems
	- The easiest way to build a great app is to be your own customer
	- Enthusiasm is an input. It’s easier to do great work on things that excite you
	- If some of them earn money, that’s a bonus
- Focus is a feature
	- An app should do one thing well. Every “yes” makes the next “no” harder
	- Bloat doesn’t arrive in one release. It arrives one reasonable feature at a time
	- [Small, focused modules](../blog/small-focused-modules/index.md) are LEGO bricks. Build big things out of small, finished pieces
- The real cost of software is not building it, it’s supporting it
	- Every feature is a promise to maintain it
	- Avoid hacks that break with the next OS update. They turn into support email
	- Don’t depend on free APIs. They go away
- Depth is the moat
	- Surface ideas get cloned in weeks. Taste, trust, and going deeper than the screenshot are hard to copy
- Native apps are better
	- Faster, smaller, and at home on the platform. Most of my apps are below 4 MB
	- Fit in, don’t stand out. The best app feels like it came with the system
- Change should earn its place
	- Redesigning for the sake of it creates work for everyone and value for no one
	- Good platform features should make packages obsolete. When Node.js made seven of mine obsolete, that was a win
- Respect the people who use your apps
	- No ads, no tracking, and no personal data
	- Pay once, own forever
	- Prices in whole dollars, not $4.99. I have no wish to manipulate my users
	- Small ethical choices compound. Your pricing reflects your values
- Ship it
	- It takes a lot of bad ideas to get to the good ones
	- You can’t predict what will be popular. Some of my most popular apps are weekend projects, and some I spent months on went nowhere. So ship more, and let reality decide
- Readability beats cleverness
	- Code is read far more often than it’s written
	- Silent on success, loud on failure
- Behind every issue and support email is a real person
	- [Empathy and kindness](../blog/empathy-in-open-source/index.md) matter as much as code
- Optimize for Monthly Happiness Percentage, not monthly recurring revenue
- The people who are crazy enough to think they can change the world are the ones who do
	- From Apple’s [“Think Different”](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Think_different) ad. It’s at the bottom of every page on this site, as a reminder that one person can still make things that matter
}

## How I Work

@Steps {
1. Write down every idea. Most of them are boring or bad, and that’s fine
1. Start with the idea, and learn what I need to make it
1. Design every app myself, and build it with AI as my tireless pair programmer
1. Support only the latest macOS and iOS, and spend the time on making the apps better instead
1. After every WWDC, go through all my apps to see how they can use the new features
	- But wait a year before trusting a brand-new API
1. Say no to features that would bring more support than value, like hacks that break with the next macOS update
1. Read every support email and reply myself
}
