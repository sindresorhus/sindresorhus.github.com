Hi, I’m Sindre ([hear how to say it](/assets/sindre-sorhus-pronunciation.m4a)). I make apps and open-source software, full time and on my own. In 2014, I quit my office job to backpack around Southeast Asia and work on open source. The backpacking ended, but the open source never did.

My time goes to [apps](/apps) for Apple devices and to open source. The apps are small, focused tools that each solve one problem well, and many of them are free. You can [read what people say about them](/apps/reviews), or [see what I’m working on now](/now).

In open source, I maintain [many npm packages](https://www.npmjs.com/~sindresorhus) and [popular projects](https://github.com/search?q=user:sindresorhus+user:chalk+user:avajs+user:xojs+archived:false&type=Repositories), from Node.js libraries to CLI tools and Swift packages, and I started [Awesome](https://github.com/sindresorhus/awesome), the lists about all kinds of interesting topics.

## Some Things I Believe

@Cards {
- I’m all in on AI
	- It’s the biggest shift in how software gets made in my lifetime, and I use it every day
	- The bottleneck is no longer writing code. It’s taste, judgment, and knowing what to build
	- With AI, I can make, verify, and ship a fix faster than I can review a pull request for it
	- I still own the result. The design, the decisions, and the quality bar are mine
- I make things for fun, and to solve my own problems
	- The easiest way to build a great app is to be your own customer
	- Enthusiasm is an input. It’s easier to do great work on things that excite you
	- Optimize for Monthly Happiness Percentage, not monthly recurring revenue. If some apps earn money, that’s a bonus
- Focus is a feature
	- An app should do one thing well. Every “yes” makes the next “no” harder
	- Bloat doesn’t arrive in one release. It arrives one reasonable feature at a time
	- Focus lets me go deeper than the screenshot. A surface idea gets cloned in weeks, but taste, trust, and depth are hard to copy
	- [Small, focused modules](../blog/small-focused-modules/index.md) are LEGO bricks. Build big things out of small, finished pieces
- One person can still make things that matter
	- From Apple’s [“Think Different”](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Think_different) ad: “The people who are crazy enough to think they can change the world are the ones who do.” It’s at the bottom of every page on this site
- The real cost of software is supporting it
	- Every feature is a promise to maintain it
	- Hacks break with the next OS update, and then they turn into support email
	- Free APIs go away, so I don’t depend on them
	- A platform feature that makes my package obsolete is a win. Node.js made seven of them obsolete
- Native apps are better
	- Faster, smaller, and at home on the platform. Most of my apps are below 4 MB
	- Fit in, don’t stand out. The best app feels like it came with the system
	- I support only the latest macOS and iOS, and spend the time on making the apps better instead
	- Redesign only when it helps. Change for the sake of change creates work for everyone and value for no one
- The people who use my apps deserve respect
	- No ads, no tracking, and no personal data
	- Pay once, own forever
	- Prices in whole dollars, not $4.99. I have no wish to manipulate my users. Small ethical choices compound
	- Behind every issue and support email is a real person. [Empathy and kindness](../blog/empathy-in-open-source/index.md) matter as much as code
- Readability beats cleverness
	- Code is read far more often than it’s written
	- Silent on success, loud on failure
}

## How I Make an App

@Steps {
1. Write down every idea. Most of them are boring or bad, and that’s fine. It takes a lot of bad ideas to get to the good ones
1. Start with the idea, and learn what I need to make it
1. Design the app myself, and build it with AI as my tireless pair programmer
1. Ship it, and let reality decide. Some of my most popular apps are weekend projects, and some I spent months on went nowhere
1. Read every support email and reply myself. More than 10,000 so far
1. After every WWDC, go through all my apps to see how they can use the new features
	- But wait a year before trusting a brand-new API
}
