Most of my apps are free. The paid ones cost a few dollars, once. You buy the app, and it’s yours, with the updates included. <!-- TODO: confirm: updates are included for all paid apps -->

People sometimes ask why I don’t do subscriptions like everyone else. This is the short answer.

## Pay one time, own forever

When you pay for something, you should get to keep it. That is how buying things worked for a very long time, and I don’t see a good reason to change it for a menu bar app that shows the week number.

A subscription makes sense when there is a cost every month, like a server. Most of my apps run entirely on your device. No account, no server, nothing that costs me money while you use it. <!-- TODO: confirm: no app needs an account or a server of mine -->

A subscription also changes the relationship. I would have to convince you every month that the app is still worth it. That pushes an app to keep adding things, and bloat doesn’t arrive in one release. It arrives one reasonable feature at a time. I would rather make a small app that does one thing well, and then keep it working.

## Whole dollars

All my paid apps are priced at whole numbers. $2, not $1.99.

I really dislike “just-below pricing”. It exists for one reason: to make you think something costs less than it does. I have no wish to manipulate my users.

It’s a tiny thing. Nobody will choose an app because the price ends in a zero. But small ethical choices compound, and your pricing choice reflects your values.

## Why charge at all?

I mainly make the apps for fun and to solve my own problems. The fact that some earn some money is just a bonus.

So why not make them all free? Support.

A free app gets a lot of downloads, and a lot of downloads means a lot of support email. Many of those people never wanted the app. They grabbed it because it was free, and then they write to ask why it does not do something it never claimed to do. A price, even a small one, means the people who get the app actually wanted it. It results in less support and a lower chance of troll reviews.

Velja is the clearest example. It was free for three years, with nearly 130K downloads. The volume of support requests became unsustainable, so I made it paid. This isn’t about revenue. It’s about managing my time by reducing the support burden to a sustainable level.

The price is not there to make me rich. It is there so that I can keep making the app.

## No in-app purchases

I’m not interested in in-app purchases. No “Pro” tier, no features unlocked one at a time, no coins. You see the price, you pay it, and you get the whole app. <!-- TODO: confirm: no current app has in-app purchases or a subscription -->

All my apps on the App Store are Universal Purchase, so you buy the app once and get it on every Apple platform it supports. They also work with Family Sharing, so one purchase covers your family.

## What I want from Apple

There are two things I have wanted from the App Store for years:

- **Time-limited trials.** The App Store has no good way to try a paid app before you buy it. So I make trials myself, outside the App Store. Most of them are the full app, with a reminder to buy it every 12 hours. See [which apps have a trial](/apps/faq#free-trial).
- **Paid upgrades.** A way to charge for a big new version, while the people who bought the old version keep it. <!-- TODO: confirm: is this how you would use paid upgrades? -->

I’m still hoping. No, the answer is not in-app purchases.

## The other side of the deal

Pay once only works if I hold up my end. So I try to:

- Keep the apps small and focused, so there is less to break.
- Support only the latest macOS and iOS, and spend that time on making the apps better. [Older versions](/apps/older-versions) stay available for older systems.
- Say no to features that would bring more support than value.
- Read every support email and reply myself.

And if a paid app is out of reach for you, check the [discounts](/apps/discounts). Students and open-source maintainers can get them.

## Most of my apps are still free

None of this means everything should cost money. Most of my apps are free, with no ads and no tracking. I love making apps, and I want them to be available to as many people as possible.

The paid ones are paid for a reason, and the reason is mostly my inbox.

Pay once, own forever. That’s the whole business model.
