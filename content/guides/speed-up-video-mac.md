@QuickAnswer {
	Open the video in the free [Gifski](/gifski) app, drag the “Speed” slider, and choose “File › Export as Video…”. The new video has no sound, so this is best for a screen recording or a clip without speech. For a video with sound, use iMovie.
}

## With Gifski

@Steps {
1. Open [Gifski](/gifski), and drop the video on its window, or choose “File › Open…”.
1. Drag the “Speed” slider, from 0.5× to 5×.
1. Optional: To trim the video, drag the yellow handles of the bar below it. To crop it, choose “Edit › Crop” (++Shift+Command+C++) to keep only a part of the screen.
1. Choose “File › Export as Video…” (++Command+E++), and click “Save”.
}

The first time you export a video with sound, Gifski says that the audio track will be ignored. Gifski opens the video formats of macOS, like MP4 and MOV.

@Tips {
- The same steps trim or crop a screen recording without a change of speed: keep the speed at 1×. [How to crop a video.](guide:crop-video-mac)
- To make a GIF of the video, click “Convert” in place of the export. [How to convert a video to a GIF.](guide:convert-video-to-gif-mac)
}

## Without an app

iMovie, the video editor of Apple, can speed up a clip with its sound ([Apple’s instructions](https://support.apple.com/guide/imovie/mov6c442b2eb/mac)):

@Steps {
1. Add the video to a project in iMovie, and select the clip in the timeline.
1. Click the Speed button to show the speed controls.
1. Choose “Fast” in the “Speed” pop-up menu, and click a speed, like 2×.
1. Choose “File › Share › File” to save the video ([Apple’s instructions](https://support.apple.com/guide/imovie/move6e0cb2ad/mac)).
}

iMovie is free in the App Store, and good for a video with sound. It is a large app with projects and a timeline, so it is slow for a quick change. To only trim a video, QuickTime Player is enough: choose “Edit › Trim…”, drag the yellow handles, click “Trim”, and save the video ([Apple’s instructions](https://support.apple.com/guide/quicktime-player/qtpf2115f6fd/mac)).
