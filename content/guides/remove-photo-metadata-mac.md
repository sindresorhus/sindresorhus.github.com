@QuickAnswer {
	++Control++-click the photo in Finder, choose “Quick Actions › Convert Image”, make sure that “Preserve Metadata” is not selected, and click the convert button. The copy has no location, date, or camera details. [Black Out](/black-out) also saves a copy without them, and can put black boxes over private parts of the photo first.
}

## With Black Out

@Steps {
1. Open the photo in [Black Out](/black-out): drop it on the window, or click “Open”. You do not have to add a black box.
1. Click “Export”, choose the format and the folder, and save. To save it in your Downloads folder at once, press ++Shift+Command+S++, or choose “Downloads” below “Quick Export” in the “File” menu.
}

The new file has no location, date, camera, or lens details. It keeps the resolution of the image. The format is PNG by default, so choose “JPEG” in the export dialog for a photo. Black Out remembers the format, also for Quick Export.

To remove the metadata of many photos at once, use “Remove All Metadata” of [Supercharge](/supercharge) in the right-click menu of Finder. [How to set it up.](guide:remove-photo-location-mac)

## Without an app

The Convert Image quick action of Finder makes a copy without metadata:

@Steps {
1. Select the photos in Finder.
1. ++Control++-click them, and choose “Quick Actions › Convert Image”.
1. Choose the format in “Format”, and make sure that “Preserve Metadata” is not selected.
1. Click the convert button, like “Convert to JPEG”.
}

macOS removes all the metadata, like the location where the photo was taken, when “Preserve Metadata” is off. The copies are saved next to the originals. This is free, and enough for most photos. Black Out is useful when you also want to hide a part of the photo, like a face or a name. To remove only the location, use Preview. [How to remove the location.](guide:remove-photo-location-mac)
