@QuickAnswer {
	Open the video in the free [Gifski](/gifski) app, click “Crop”, drag the frame, and choose “File › Export as Video…”. The new video has no sound, so this is best for a screen recording. For a video with sound, crop it in Photos.
}

## With Gifski

@Steps {
1. Open [Gifski](/gifski), and drop the video on its window, or choose “File › Open…”.
1. Click “Crop” in the toolbar, or choose “Edit › Crop” (++Shift+Command+C++).
1. Drag the edges of the frame. For a fixed shape, choose a ratio in the menu next to the button, like 16:9, 1:1, or 9:16, or choose “Custom” to type a ratio or a size in pixels.
1. Click the check mark to end the crop.
1. Choose “File › Export as Video…” (++Command+E++), and click “Save”.
}

Hold ++Shift++ while you drag to scale both sides, and ++Option++ to resize from the center. Gifski saves the video as MP4, and drops the sound. It can also trim the video and change its speed. [How to speed up a video.](guide:speed-up-video-mac)

## Without an app

Photos crops a video and keeps the sound ([Apple’s instructions](https://support.apple.com/guide/photos/pht13f0918f0/mac)):

@Steps {
1. Drag the video into Photos, and double-click it.
1. Click “Edit”, and then click “Crop” in the toolbar.
1. Drag the selection rectangle, or click an aspect ratio in the sidebar. Then click “Done”.
1. To save a copy as a file, choose “File › Export › Export 1 Video…”.
}

The crop changes the video in your photo library, but you can go back to the original. QuickTime Player can trim and rotate a video, but it cannot crop it. Photos is the better choice for a video with sound, and Gifski is faster for a screen recording that you do not keep in your library.
