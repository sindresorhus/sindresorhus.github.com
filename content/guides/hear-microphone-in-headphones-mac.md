@QuickAnswer {
	Click [Recordia](../apps/recordia/index.md) in the menu bar, and choose “Monitor Input”. You hear your microphone in your headphones at once, also when you do not record. Without an app, the audio recording window of QuickTime Player plays the microphone when you turn up its volume.
}

## With Recordia

@Steps {
1. Connect your headphones. If you use the speakers, the microphone hears itself, and you get feedback.
1. Click the [Recordia](../apps/recordia/index.md) icon in the menu bar. Below “Audio Source”, choose “Microphone”, and below “Input Device”, choose your microphone.
1. Choose “Monitor Input”. If macOS asks, allow access to the microphone.
1. To stop, click the icon, and choose “Stop Input Monitoring”.
}

The sound plays on the default output device of the Mac. To turn monitoring on and off from any app, set a keyboard shortcut for “Toggle input monitoring” in the “Shortcuts” settings. To hear the microphone each time that you record, choose “Monitor Input While Recording” in the menu.

Monitoring works only with the “Microphone” audio source, not with “System Audio” or “Microphone + System Audio”. [How to record audio.](record-audio-mac.md)

## Without an app

QuickTime Player can play the microphone in its recording window:

@Steps {
1. Connect your headphones.
1. Open QuickTime Player, and choose “File › New Audio Recording”.
1. Drag the volume control in the window to the right to hear the microphone.
}

[Apple’s instructions.](https://support.apple.com/guide/quicktime-player/qtpf25d6f827/mac) Close the window to stop. It works only while the window is open, so it is good for a quick test of the microphone. Recordia monitors from the menu bar while you use other apps, and starts with a keyboard shortcut.
