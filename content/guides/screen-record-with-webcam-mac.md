@QuickAnswer {
	Open [Camera Preview](/camera-preview), and choose “Window › Stay on Top” to keep the camera above your other windows. Then press ++Shift+Command+5++, and record the screen. The camera window is in the recording. [Without an app](#without-an-app), a new movie recording of QuickTime Player can show the camera in a window that floats on top.
}

## With Camera Preview

@Steps {
1. Open [Camera Preview](/camera-preview). A window without a title bar shows the camera. To use another camera, press ++Command+1++, ++Command+2++, and so on.
1. Choose “Window › Stay on Top”, or press ++Control+Command+T++, so that the camera stays above the windows that you record.
1. Make the window small, and drag the image to move the window to a corner of the screen.
1. Press ++Shift+Command+5++, and click “Record Entire Screen” or “Record Selected Portion”. To record your voice, click “Options”, and choose a microphone.
1. Click “Record”. To stop, click the stop button in the menu bar, or press ++Control+Command+Esc++.
}

Do not use “Record Selected Window”, as it records only one window, without the camera. To see yourself like in a mirror, choose “Camera › Flip Horizontally”. To show the camera also over an app in full screen, open the settings, and in “Show in”, choose “Menu Bar”. [How to see yourself on the camera.](guide:see-camera-mac)

## Without an app

QuickTime Player can show the camera ([Apple’s instructions](https://support.apple.com/102618)):

@Steps {
1. Open QuickTime Player, and choose “File › New Movie Recording”. Do not click the record button.
1. Choose “View › Float on Top”, so the camera window stays in front of the windows that you click.
1. Record the screen with ++Shift+Command+5++.
}

The screen recording of macOS itself has no option to show the camera. Camera Preview has no window frame or controls on the camera, and can stay on top also over an app in full screen.
