@QuickAnswer {
	The wallpaper settings of macOS have pictures, colors, and aerials, but no animated GIF. Add the link of the GIF to the free [Plash](../apps/plash/index.md) app, and the animated GIF fills the screen behind your windows and desktop icons.
}

## With Plash

@Steps {
1. Copy the link of the GIF. It must be the link of the image itself, which usually ends in `.gif`, not the link of a page that shows it. In a browser, Control-click the GIF, and choose “Copy Image Address” or “Copy Image Link”.
1. Open [Plash](../apps/plash/index.md). Its icon is in the menu bar.
1. Click the icon, and choose “Add Website…”.
1. Paste the link in “URL”, and click “Add”.
}

Plash makes the GIF fill the screen, and crops the edges when the shape is different from the screen. So a large GIF with the shape of your screen looks best.

@Tips {
- An animated wallpaper uses power. On a MacBook, open the Plash settings, click “Advanced”, and turn on “Deactivate while on battery”.
- Add several GIFs, and switch between them in the menu.
- For a GIF file on your Mac, put it in a folder together with a file named `index.html` that shows the GIF. When you add the website, click “Local Website…”, and choose the folder.
}

Plash can show any website as the wallpaper, not only a GIF. See [how to use a website as your wallpaper](website-wallpaper-mac.md).

## Without an app

macOS has no animated GIF wallpaper. In “[System Settings › Wallpaper](x-apple.systempreferences:com.apple.Wallpaper-Settings.extension)”, you can choose a dynamic wallpaper, which changes during the day, or an aerial, which moves slowly when you lock or unlock the Mac. [Apple’s instructions.](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/mchlp3013/mac)

These are good when you only want some motion. To use your own animation, you need an app.
