@QuickAnswer {
	Click [Text Lens](/text-lens) in the menu bar, choose “Capture”, and select the table on the screen. It copies the text with a tab between the cells and a new line for each row, so it pastes into the cells of Numbers, Excel, or Google Sheets. [Without an app](#without-an-app), Live Text in Preview copies the text of an image.
}

## With Text Lens

@Steps {
1. Show the table on the screen, like in an image, a PDF, or a video.
1. Click the [Text Lens](/text-lens) icon in the menu bar, and choose “Capture”.
1. Drag over the table.
1. Click the first cell in the spreadsheet, and press ++Command+V++.
}

The text recognition runs on your Mac, so it also works offline. Check the numbers after you paste, as a blurry or small table can give wrong characters.

@Tips {
- For an image file, click the icon, and choose “File…” below “Import From”. For an image on the clipboard, choose “Clipboard”.
- To capture with a keyboard shortcut, set “Capture” in the settings. Or turn on “Click menu bar icon to capture”.
- To see the text before you paste it, set “Action” to “Show in Window” in the settings.
}

## Without an app

Live Text in Preview copies the text of an image:

@Steps {
1. Open the image in Preview.
1. Drag over the table to select the text. If you cannot select it, choose “Tools › Text Selection”.
1. ++Control++-click the selection, and choose “Copy Text”.
}

[Apple’s instructions.](https://support.apple.com/guide/preview/prvw625a5b2c/mac) Apple does not say how Live Text copies the rows and the columns of a table, so check the result in the spreadsheet. For text on the screen, take a screenshot first. [How to copy text from the screen.](guide:copy-text-from-screen-mac)
