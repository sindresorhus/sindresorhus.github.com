@QuickAnswer {
	Click [Recordia](/recordia) in the menu bar, choose your microphone, and choose “Record”. The menu bar shows the audio level while you talk. Stop after some seconds, and listen to the recording. [Without an app](#without-an-app), open “[System Settings › Sound](x-apple.systempreferences:com.apple.Sound-Settings.extension)”, click “Input”, and talk: the “Input level” moves when the microphone hears you.
}

## With Recordia

@Steps {
1. Click the [Recordia](/recordia) icon in the menu bar. Below “Audio Source”, choose “Microphone”, and below “Input Device”, choose the microphone to test.
1. Click the icon again, choose “Record”, and talk. The icon in the menu bar becomes a live audio level. If it stays flat, the Mac gets no sound from the microphone.
1. After some seconds, click the icon, and choose “Stop”. Then choose where to save the recording.
1. To listen, click the icon, choose “Previous Recording › Show in Finder”, and press ++Space++ to play the file.
}

A recording lets you hear how you sound to others, like the noise in the room, and if the microphone is too far away. Some headsets, like AirPods, can give silence even when the Mac uses them, and the live level shows this at once.

To hear yourself live in headphones, without a recording, choose “Monitor Input” in the menu. [How to hear your microphone in your headphones.](guide:hear-microphone-in-headphones-mac)

## Without an app

@Steps {
1. Open “[System Settings › Sound](x-apple.systempreferences:com.apple.Sound-Settings.extension)”.
1. Click “Input”, and select your microphone. To use another microphone, see [how to change the microphone](guide:change-microphone-mac). <source:https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/mchlp2567/mac>
1. Talk. The “Input level” bar moves when the microphone hears you. If it is too low, drag “Input volume” to the right.
}

This is the fastest check that the microphone works. To hear how you sound, record a memo in the Voice Memos app, and play it.
