@QuickAnswer {
	Open the video in the free [Gifski](/gifski) app, trim it to a short moment, turn on “Bounce” next to “Loops”, and click “Convert”. For a Live Photo, the Photos app can do it: choose the “Bounce” effect, and then “File › Export › Export GIF”.
}

## With Gifski

@Steps {
1. Open [Gifski](/gifski), and drop the video on its window, or click “Open”.
1. Trim the video to the moment that you want, like one to three seconds.
1. Next to “Loops”, turn on “Bounce”. The preview now plays forward and then backward.
1. Click “Convert”, and then click “Save”.
}

The GIF plays the frames forward and then backward, so it is about two times as long and as large as the same GIF without “Bounce”. To make the file smaller, lower the dimensions, the frame rate, or the quality. “Forever” is on by default, so the GIF loops without end.

## Without an app

The Photos app can make a boomerang GIF from a Live Photo ([Apple’s instructions](https://support.apple.com/guide/photos/phtfd7a526c7/mac)):

@Steps {
1. In Photos, double-click the Live Photo, and click “Edit”.
1. Click the pop-up menu at the bottom right of the photo, and choose “Bounce”. Then click “Done”.
1. Choose “File › Export › Export GIF”, and choose where to save it.
}

This works only for a Live Photo. For a video, like a screen recording or a clip from your camera, use Gifski.
