@QuickAnswer {
	Pause the video, click [Text Lens](/text-lens) in the menu bar, choose “Capture”, and drag over the text. The text is copied. [Without an app](#without-an-app), pause the video in Safari or QuickTime Player, and select the text with Live Text.
}

## With Text Lens

@Steps {
1. Pause the video where the text shows, like a slide of a talk on YouTube. It can be in any browser or player, like Chrome or VLC.
1. Click the [Text Lens](/text-lens) icon in the menu bar, and choose “Capture”.
1. Drag over the text. The text is copied. Paste it with ++Command+V++.
}

To collect the text of many slides, open the settings, and below “After Capture”, choose “Show in Window”. While the window is open, the text of each new capture is added to the text in it. Then click “Copy Text” to copy all of it.

Set a keyboard shortcut for “Capture” in the settings, so you do not have to go to the menu bar for each slide. The text recognition runs on your Mac, so it also works offline. [How to copy text from an image or the screen.](guide:copy-text-from-screen-mac)

Some streaming services block screenshots of their videos. Then the capture is black, and there is no text to copy.

## Without an app

macOS has Live Text, which finds the text in a paused video in Safari and QuickTime Player:

@Steps {
1. Open the video in Safari or QuickTime Player, and pause it where the text shows.
1. Drag over the text to select it.
1. Press ++Command+C++.
}

Live Text does not work in videos in Chrome, Firefox, or most other apps. There, take a screenshot of the paused video with ++Shift+Command+4++, open it in Preview, and select the text.

This is enough for a video in Safari. Text Lens works the same way in all apps, and collects the text of many slides in one place.
